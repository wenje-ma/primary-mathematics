> **Definition 11.5.1 (圆各部分的名称)**
> 
> 圆规针尖固定的一点叫圆心, 用 $O$ 表示; 连接圆心和圆上任意一点的线段叫半径, 用 $r$ 表示; 通过圆心并且两端都在圆上的线段叫直径, 用 $d$ 表示. 同一个圆里 $d=2r$. 墨子云: "圆, 一中同长也".

> **Definition 11.5.2 (弧, 扇形, 圆心角)**
> 
> 圆上两点之间的部分叫弧; 一条弧和经过这条弧两端的两条半径所围成的图形叫扇形; 顶点在圆心的角叫圆心角. 同一个圆中, 扇形的大小与圆心角的大小有关.

> **Definition 11.5.3 (圆周率)**
> 
> 任意一个圆的周长除以它的直径, 所得的商是一个固定的数, 叫圆周率, 用 $\pi$ 表示. 它是无限不循环小数, 计算时通常取 $\pi\approx3.14$.

> **Definition 11.5.4 (圆的周长)**
> 
> 围成圆的曲线的长叫圆的周长, $C=\pi{d}=2\pi{r}$.

> **Definition 11.5.5 (圆的面积)**
> 
> 圆所占平面的大小叫圆的面积. 把圆等分拼成近似长方形, 长为 $\pi{r}$, 宽为 $r$, 故 $S=\pi{r}^2$.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Integrated Problem 5) A wall clock's minute hand is $20\;\text{cm}$; after $30$ minutes, how far has the tip traveled? After $45$?

### Solution to Exercise 1

The tip's full circle is $2\times3.14\times20=125.6\;\text{cm}$. $30$ minutes walks half a circle $=62.8\;\text{cm}$; $45$ minutes walks $\frac{3}{4}$ circle $=94.2\;\text{cm}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Integrated Problem 6) A circular cattle pen radius $15\;\text{m}$; at least how much wire to wrap it $3$ times? If posts are every $2\;\text{m}$, about how many?

### Solution to Exercise 2

One wrap $2\times3.14\times15=94.2\;\text{m}$, $3$ wraps $282.6\;\text{m}$. Posts $94.2\div2\approx47$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Integrated Problem 7) Fill: (1) largest circle in a square $r=2$; square perimeter $\_\_$, circle perimeter $\_\_$; (2) rectangle with two equal circles $r=1.5$; one circle perimeter $\_\_$, rectangle perimeter $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

(1) Square side $=d=4$, perimeter $16\;\text{cm}$; circle perimeter $3.14\times4=12.56\;\text{cm}$. (2) Circle perimeter $2\times3.14\times1.5=9.42\;\text{cm}$; rectangle length $=4r=6$, width $=2r=3$, perimeter $\left(6+3\right)\times2=18\;\text{cm}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Integrated Problem 8) Inside a square paper of perimeter $100\;\text{cm}$, cut the largest-radius circle; what is its radius?

### Solution to Exercise 4

Square side $=100\div4=25\;\text{cm}$; the largest circle's diameter equals the side $25\;\text{cm}$, radius $12.5\;\text{cm}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Integrated Problem 9) A window has a decorative light of the shown shape, a semicircle on a square, $d=50\;\text{cm}$. About how many meters of lights?

### Solution to Exercise 5

Light length $=$ semicircle $+$ three sides $=\frac{1}{2}\times3.14\times50+50\times3=78.5+150=228.5\;\text{cm}=2.285\;\text{m}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Extension Problem 12) Bind cylindrical objects into different shapes (ignore knots); how much rope each group? What do you find?

### Solution to Exercise 8

Rope length $=$ one full circumference $+$ some diameters. As circles increase, the straight parts grow, but the arc parts still sum to one full circle.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Example 5) A circle of radius $10\;\text{cm}$. First estimate area by counting squares, then cut into equal sectors and rearrange into an approximate rectangle of length $\pi{r}$, width $r$. What is the area formula?

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 11.5.5 (Area of a Circle)**, rectangle area $=$ length $\times$ width $=\pi{r}\times{r}=\pi{r}^2$, so $S=\pi{r}^2$. The more sectors, the closer to a rectangle.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Example 6) A wind turbine rotor radius is $123\;\text{m}$; its swept area? A standard football pitch is $105\;\text{m}$ by $68\;\text{m}$; about how many pitches? (Round.)

### Solution to Exercise 10

By **Definition 11.5.5 (Area of a Circle)**: $S=3.14\times123^2=3.14\times15129\approx47505\;\text{m}^2$. Pitch $105\times68=7140\;\text{m}^2$, $47505\div7140\approx7$ pitches.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Do It Problem 1) A round coffee table is $1\;\text{m}$ diameter; its area? A circular reservoir perimeter about $31.4\;\text{m}$; about what area?

### Solution to Exercise 11

Table $r=0.5\;\text{m}$, $S=3.14\times0.5^2=0.785\;\text{m}^2$. Reservoir $r=31.4\div3.14\div2=5\;\text{m}$, $S=3.14\times25=78.5\;\text{m}^2$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Example 7) A jade ring \"Yu-embossed silkworm-pattern bi\" is an annulus: outer diameter $26.3\;\text{cm}$, inner $5.4\;\text{cm}$. Its area? (One decimal.)

### Solution to Exercise 12

Annulus area $=$ outer $-$ inner $=3.14\times\left(\frac{26.3}{2}\right)^2-3.14\times\left(\frac{5.4}{2}\right)^2=3.14\times\left(13.15^2-2.7^2\right)=3.14\times165.6325\approx520.1\;\text{cm}^2$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Example 8) Find the shaded area: (1) a large semicircle minus a small plus a small (radius $5\;\text{cm}$); (2) a rectangle $r=3\;\text{cm}$, upper semicircle moved below.

### Solution to Exercise 13

(1) $3.14\times5^2\div2=39.25\;\text{cm}^2$; (2) translate the upper semicircle down; the shaded part is exactly the rectangle $6\times3=18\;\text{cm}^2$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Do It) Find shaded areas (unit: cm): (1) annulus inner radius $4$, width $2$; (2) rectangle $2+1+2$ with shaded semicircle; (3) concentric-circle red annular sector.

### Solution to Exercise 14

(1) Outer radius $6$, $3.14\times\left(6^2-4^2\right)=3.14\times20=62.8\;\text{cm}^2$; (2) rectangle length $5$, width $2$; shaded pieces form a rectangle $3\times2=6\;\text{cm}^2$; (3) use the annular-sector formula $\frac{n}{360}\pi\left(R^2-r^2\right)$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Example 9) Study the relation between square and circle in \"square-outside circle-inside\" and \"circle-outside square-inside\". Let the circle radius be $a$.

### Solution to Exercise 15

\"Square-outside circle-inside\": square side $=d=2a$, area $4a^2$, circle area $\pi{a}^2$, circle is $\frac{\pi}{4}$ of the square. \"Circle-outside square-inside\": view the square as two triangles, area $2a^2$, circle is $\frac{\pi}{2}$ of it. Overlaying, the large square is $2$ times the small.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Area of a Circle, Do It) Given the square's area is $20\;\text{cm}^2$, what is the circle's area? The shaded area?

### Solution to Exercise 16

By **Exercise 170**, the circle is $\frac{\pi}{4}$ of the square: circle $20\times\frac{\pi}{4}=5\pi\approx15.7\;\text{cm}^2$; shaded $20-15.7=4.3\;\text{cm}^2$.

$\blacksquare$
