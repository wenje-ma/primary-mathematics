> **Definition 1.1.1 (基数)**<br>一个数可以表示一类事物的个数, 如 $1$ 表示 $1$ 栋房子, $5$ 表示 $5$ 只鸭子. 数到几, 就有几个.
> **Definition 1.1.2 (Equal, More, Less)**<br>用一一对应比较两类物体的多少: 一一对应后都对应完, 称为同样多; 有剩余的称为多, 另一类称为少.
> **Definition 1.1.3 (等号,大于号,小于号)**<br>表示两个数大小关系的符号: 等号 "$=$" (读作"等于"), 大于号 "$> $" (读作"大于"), 小于号 "$< $" (读作"小于"), 开口朝向较大的数.
> **Definition 1.1.4 (基数与序数)**<br>"有几个"表示数量多少, 是基数; "第几个"表示排列的位置, 是序数. 如 $5$ 人排队, "有 $5$ 人"是基数, "排第 $2$"是序数.
> **Definition 1.1.5 (数的分与合)**<br>一个数可以分成两个数, 两个数也可以组成这个数. 如 $5$ 可以分成 $1$ 和 $4$, $2$ 和 $3$, $3$ 和 $2$, $4$ 和 $1$; 反之这些数对都可以组成 $5$.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Understanding 1–5, Do It, Problem 1) Circle as shown: circle out $5$ cucumbers, $3$ eggplants, $2$ tomatoes, and $4$ green peppers respectively.

### Solution to Exercise 1

Following "as shown", circle the objects in each group whose quantity matches the number on the left, i.e. circle out $5$ cucumbers, $3$ eggplants, $2$ tomatoes, and $4$ green peppers respectively. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Understanding 1–5, Do It, Problem 2) Match the numbers: connect the numbers $1\sim5$ to the corresponding quantities of objects ($1$ book, $2$ shuttlecocks, $3$ badminton shuttlecocks, $4$ boxes of crayons, $5$ crayons).

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 1.1.1 (Cardinal Number)**, the numbers $1,2,3,4,5$ represent $1,2,3,4,5$ objects respectively. Thus $1$ connects to $1$ book, $2$ to $2$ shuttlecocks, $3$ to $3$ badminton shuttlecocks, $4$ to $4$ boxes of crayons, and $5$ to $5$ crayons. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 3

## Exercise 9

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Comparing Size, Tug of War) There are $3$ people on the left and $5$ people on the right in a tug of war. Is this game fair? Say how to make it fair.

### Solution to Exercise 12

It is not fair because the two sides have different numbers of people. By **Definition 1.1.2 (Same Amount, More, Fewer)**, make the two sides have the same number of people; for example, increase the left side to $5$ people, or reduce the right side to $3$ people, or adjust both sides so the numbers are equal. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Comparing Size, Thinking Question) One monkey has $3$ peaches in front of it, and the other also has $3$ peaches in front of it with more peaches still in the basket. It says "my peaches are more than yours." Is it definitely right?

### Solution to Exercise 13

Not necessarily. By **Definition 1.1.2 (Same Amount, More, Fewer)**, its peaches are "the ones in the basket plus the $3$ displayed"; it is truly more than the other only when the number of peaches in the basket is greater than $0$. If the basket is empty, they are the same amount. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Which Position, Do It, Problem 1) (1) Counting from the left, circle out $4$ birds and color the $4$th one. (2) The $2$nd bird counting from the right flies away. There are $\_\_$ birds left.

### Solution to Exercise 14

(1) By **Definition 1.1.4 (Cardinal Number and Ordinal Number)**, counting from left to right, circle the first $4$ birds and color the $4$th from the left. (2) There were originally $5$ birds; $1$ flies away, so $4$ remain. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Which Position, Do It, Problem 2) There are $\_\_$ people in total; counting from the left, $\_\_$ is at position $\_\_$; do the numbers in the two boxes mean the same thing?

### Solution to Exercise 15

The family in the picture has $4$ people in total; counting from the left, Mom is at position $2$. By **Definition 1.1.4 (Cardinal Number and Ordinal Number)**, one box tells "how many people in total" (cardinal) and the other tells "which position" (ordinal); they do not mean the same thing. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Decomposition and Composition, Try It) $4$ red flowers are split into two piles. How can they be split? Place them.

### Solution to Exercise 16

By **Definition 1.1.5 (Decomposition and Composition of Numbers)**, $4=1+3$, $4=2+2$, $4=3+1$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 17

(Lesson: Decomposition and Composition, Do It, Problem 1) There are $3$ $\triangle$ in total: $1$ in the left hand and $2$ in the right hand. The other hand has $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 17

By **Definition 1.1.5 (Decomposition and Composition of Numbers)**, $3-1=2$, so the other hand has $2$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 18

(Lesson: Decomposition and Composition, Do It, Problem 2) Place and fill: $3$ and what make $5$? $1$ and what make $2$? $2$ and what make $3$? What and $3$ make $4$? Which two numbers make $5$?

### Solution to Exercise 18

By **Definition 1.1.5 (Decomposition and Composition of Numbers)**: $3+2=5$; $1+1=2$; $2+1=3$; $1+3=4$; $1$ and $4$, $2$ and $3$, $3$ and $2$, $4$ and $1$ make $5$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 19

(Lesson: Decomposition and Composition, Do It, Problem 3) $5$ birds want to fly into two birdhouses. Will each birdhouse get the same number?

### Solution to Exercise 19

No. By **Definition 1.1.5 (Decomposition and Composition of Numbers)**, $5$ is odd and cannot be split into two equal numbers; a total that can be split evenly must be even. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 20

### Solution to Exercise 20
