> **Definition 10.7.1** (Rotation) <br>An object turning about a point (or an axis) is rotation. To describe rotation, state three elements: the point (center of rotation), the direction (clockwise or counterclockwise), and the angle. Rotation does not change a figure's shape or size, only its position and orientation.
> 
> **Definition 10.7.2** (Features of Rotation) <br>After a figure rotates about a point, the center stays fixed; every edge of the figure rotates about that point by the same angle in the same direction, and corresponding points keep the same distance from the center.

### Exercise 122

(Lesson: Rotation, Do It) On a clock face, the hand turns from "12" to "1" about point O clockwise by how many degrees? From "1" to "3"? From "3" to "6"? From "6" to "12"?

### Solution to Exercise 122

By **Definition 10.7.1** (Rotation), the clock face is $360^\circ$, split by 12 major marks, so each mark is $30^\circ$. From "12" to "1": $30^\circ$; from "1" to "3", 2 marks, $60^\circ$; from "3" to "6", 3 marks, $90^\circ$; from "6" to "12", 6 marks, $180^\circ$. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 123

(Lesson: Rotation, Do It) When a car passes on the left, the barrier arm rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise about point $O_1$; when a car passes on the right, the barrier arm rotates about point $\_\_$ in a $\_\_$ direction by $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 123

By **Definition 10.7.1** (Rotation), when a car passes on the right, the arm rotates $90^\circ$ counterclockwise about point $O_2$ (symmetric with the left). $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 124

(Lesson: Rotation, Example 3) Draw triangle AOB after a $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation about point O.

### Solution to Exercise 124

By **Definition 10.7.2** (Features of Rotation), the center O is fixed; first draw $A'$ with $OA'\perp OA$ and $OA'=OA$; then draw $B'$ with $OB'\perp OB$ and $OB'=OB$; finally join $O,A',B'$ in order to get the rotated triangle. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 126

### Solution to Exercise 126

By **Definition 10.7.1** (Rotation), a pendulum swings left and right, at each pass rotating no more than $10^\circ$ counterclockwise (or clockwise, depending on direction) about O; a windmill rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise (or counterclockwise) about O. ### Exercise 127

(Lesson: Exercise 21, Problem 3) Figure OABC rotates $90^\circ$ clockwise about O to get Figure 2; how does it rotate about O to get Figure 3?

### Solution to Exercise 127

By **Definition 10.7.2** (Features of Rotation), observe Figure 3's position relative to the original; if it rotated $180^\circ$, answer: Figure 3 is obtained by rotating $180^\circ$ clockwise (or counterclockwise) about O. $\blacksquare$
