> **Definition 9.4.3 (旋转)**
>

> **Definition 9.8.1 (确定事件与不确定事件)**
> 
> 在一定条件下, 一定发生的事件叫必然事件 (如太阳从东边升起), 一定不发生的事件叫不可能事件 (如太阳从西边升起), 可能发生也可能不发生的事件叫随机事件或不确定事件 (如抽签抽到什么节目).

> **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**
> 
> 随机事件发生的可能性有大有小. 在总数中所占数量越多, 发生的可能性越大; 所占数量越少, 发生的可能性越小.

> **Definition 9.8.3 (根据试验数据推测)**
> 
> 通过重复试验记录数据, 根据数据中各结果出现的次数多少, 可以推测盒子中哪种颜色的球多, 哪种少. 试验次数越多, 推测越可靠.

> **Definition 9.9.1 (小数乘除法的整理)**
> 
> 小数乘法转化为整数乘法计算, 再看乘数共几位小数就从积右边数几位点小数点; 小数除法先把除数转化为整数再除. 乘除法都可转化为整数运算, 运用积的变化规律和商不变规律.

> **Definition 9.9.2 (图形与几何的整理)**
> 
> 从前面, 上面, 左面三个方向观察组合体可还原立体图形; 轴对称对应点到对称轴距离相等; 平移和旋转不改变图形形大小; 平行四边形面积 $S=ah$, 三角形 $S=ah\div2$, 梯形 $S=\left(a+b\right)h\div2$.

> **Definition 9.9.3 (可能性的整理)**
> 
> 事件分为必然事件, 不可能事件和随机事件; 随机事件发生的可能性有大有小, 可通过试验数据推测.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Likelihood, Example 1) Three students draw lots to perform; three cards read sing, dance, and recite. (1) Xiaoming draws first; what might he draw? (2) After Xiaoming draws dance, what might Xiaoli draw? (3) After Xiaoli draws recite, what will Xiaohong draw?

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 9.8.1 (Certain and Uncertain Events)**: (1) All three are possible — sing, dance, or recite. (2) Dance is impossible; it may be sing or recite. (3) Only sing remains, so Xiaohong must draw sing.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Likelihood, Do It, Problem 1) Drawing chips from two boxes: (1) Which box definitely yields a red chip? (2) Which box might yield a green chip? (3) Which box cannot yield a green chip?

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 9.8.1 (确定事件与不确定事件)**, a box of all red chips definitely yields a red chip; a box containing green chips might yield a green chip; a box with no green chips cannot yield a green chip.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Likelihood, Example 2) A box contains red and blue chips. Taking turns, draw one, record its color, replace it, and shake well; repeat $20$ times. Red came up $17$ times and blue $3$ times. On the next draw, which color is more likely?

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 9.8.2 (Magnitude of Likelihood)**, red chips were drawn far more often than blue, so we infer there are more red chips in the box, and red is more likely on the next draw.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Likelihood, Do It, Problem 2) Draw one chip from the box; what colors might it be? Which color is most likely? Which least?

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, the box may yield as many colors as it contains; the most numerous color is most likely and the least numerous is least likely.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Likelihood, Example 3) A group draws balls; red comes up $15$ times and yellow $5$ times. Based on the data, which balls are more numerous in the box?

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 9.8.3 (Inferring from Experimental Data)**, red balls were drawn far more often than yellow, so we infer there are more red balls and fewer yellow balls.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Lesson: Likelihood, Do It, Problem 3) A group's ball-drawing record: red $12$ times, blue $9$ times, yellow $3$ times. What inference can be drawn? Xiaoliang infers that there are fewer yellow balls than blue balls. Is he right?

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Definition 9.8.3 (根据试验数据推测)**, the most frequent (red) suggests red balls are most numerous, and the least frequent (yellow) suggests yellow balls are fewest. Xiaoliang's direction is correct, but a single trial may contain error; after many trials the inference is more reliable.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Review, Problem 1) Draw one ball from a box; what is the result? Match: must draw yellow; must draw blue; may draw yellow; may draw blue; cannot draw blue; cannot draw red.

### Solution to Exercise 7

By **Definition 9.8.1 (确定事件与不确定事件)**, an all-yellow box matches "must draw yellow"; an all-blue box matches "must draw blue"; a box with both yellow and blue matches "may draw yellow" and "may draw blue"; a box with no blue matches "cannot draw blue"; a box with no red matches "cannot draw red".

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Review, Problem 2) Spin the pointer; on which color is it more likely to stop? Less likely?

### Solution to Exercise 8

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, the larger the area of a color, the more likely the pointer stops there; the smaller the area, the less likely.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Review, Problem 3) Among $9$ cards, $5$ tell stories, $3$ are songs, and $1$ is a dance. Drawing one card, what is most likely?

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, story cards are most numerous ($5$), so drawing a story is most likely.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Comprehensive, Problem 1) A cube has its six faces labeled $1\sim6$. Rolling once, which numbers may come up? Between odd and even, which is more likely?

### Solution to Exercise 10

It may roll $1,2,3,4,5,6$. Odd numbers are $1,3,5$, $3$ in total; even numbers are $2,4,6$, $3$ in total; the likelihoods are equal.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Comprehensive, Problem 2) A box has red, yellow, and white balls; drawing $60$ times gives red $36$, yellow $20$, white $4$. Infer which balls are most and least numerous.

### Solution to Exercise 11

By **Definition 9.8.3 (根据试验数据推测)**, red is most frequent ($36$ times), so red balls are most numerous; white is least frequent ($4$ times), so white balls are fewest.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Comprehensive, Problem 3) Color as required: the draw must be a red ball; the draw cannot be a red ball; the draw may be a red ball.

### Solution to Exercise 12

By **Definition 9.8.1 (确定事件与不确定事件)**, coloring everything red means a red ball must be drawn; coloring nothing red means a red ball cannot be drawn; coloring partly red and partly other colors means a red ball may be drawn.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 19, Extension) Place $10$ cards in a bag: drawing the number "1" should be most likely, "5" least likely, and "3" and "7" equally likely. How should the card numbers be filled in?

### Solution to Exercise 13

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, let "1" be most numerous (e.g. $4$ cards), "5" least (e.g. $1$ card), and "3" and "7" equal (e.g. $2.5$ cards each, rounded to $2$ each, or $1.5$ cards each, rounded), distributing the rest. For example: four cards of $1$, two of $3$, two of $7$, two of $5$, totaling $10$ cards.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Rolling and Tossing, Example) Roll two dice at once; what sums are possible? The teacher wins with $5,6,7,8,9$; the students win with $2,3,4,10,11,12$. Is the game fair?

### Solution to Exercise 14

The sum of two dice may be $2\sim12$. A sum of $2$ has only $\left(1,1\right)$; a sum of $3$ has $\left(1,2\right)\left(2,1\right)$, two ways; a sum of $4$ has $3$ ways; $5$ has $4$ ways; $6$ has $5$ ways; $7$ has $6$ ways; $8$ has $5$ ways; $9$ has $4$ ways; $10$ has $3$ ways; $11$ has $2$ ways; $12$ has $1$ way. The teacher wins in $4+5+6+5+4=24$ ways, and the students in $1+2+3+3+2+1=12$ ways; it is not fair, and the teacher is more likely to win.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 20, Review, Problem 1) Color the spinner as required: (1) the pointer may stop on red, yellow, or blue; (2) it may stop on all three, with blue most likely and red least likely.

### Solution to Exercise 15

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, (1) color a part of each of the three colors. (2) Make the blue region largest, the red region smallest, and yellow in between.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 20, Comprehensive) Paint a cube's faces red and blue so that rolling a red face up is more likely than blue. How can it be painted?

### Solution to Exercise 16

By **Definition 9.8.2 (可能性的大小)**, make the number of red faces exceed the blue faces, e.g. red on $4$ faces and blue on $2$, or red on $5$ faces and blue on $1$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 17

(Lesson: Likelihood, Exercise 20, Comprehensive) Judge: (1) If the first coin toss lands heads, the second must land tails. (2) The sum of two numbers cannot exceed their product. (3) A triangle's interior angle sum may be $179^\circ$. (4) For a fixed perimeter, a square's area must exceed a rectangle's.

### Solution to Exercise 17

(1) False; each toss may be heads or tails independently. (2) False; e.g. $1+1=2>1\times1=1$. (3) False; a triangle's interior angle sum must be $180^\circ$. (4) True; for a fixed perimeter, the closer length and width are, the larger the area, and a square (length equal to width) has the largest area.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 18

(Lesson: Review and Connections, Application, Problem 1) Compute and compare: $27\times3$ vs $2.7\times3$ vs $2.7\times0.3$ vs $2.7\times0.03$; $24\div6$ vs $2.4\div6$ vs $2.4\div0.6$ vs $2.4\div0.06$.

### Solution to Exercise 18

By **Definition 9.9.1 (Review of Decimal Multiplication and Division)**: $27\times3=81$, $2.7\times3=8.1$, $2.7\times0.3=0.81$, $2.7\times0.03=0.081$; $24\div6=4$, $2.4\div6=0.4$, $2.4\div0.6=4$, $2.4\div0.06=40$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 19

(Lesson: Review and Connections, Application, Problem 2) Mom spends $10$ yuan on $2.5$ kg of oranges; apples cost $1.6$ times as much per kg as oranges. How much does $3$ kg of apples cost?

### Solution to Exercise 19

Oranges cost $10\div2.5=4$ yuan/kg. Apples cost $4\times1.6=6.4$ yuan/kg. $3$ kg of apples costs $6.4\times3=19.2$ yuan.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 20

(Lesson: Review and Connections, Application, Problem 3) Uncle Wang processes $a$ parts per hour, and in $t$ hours processes $c$ parts in total. (1) At $30$ parts per hour, how many does he process in $t$ hours? (2) At $25$ parts per hour, how many hours to process $100$ parts?

### Solution to Exercise 20

By **Definition 9.5.1 (Expressing Numbers with Letters)**, $c=at$. (1) $30t$ parts. (2) $25t=100$, $t=4$ hours.

$\blacksquare$
