> **Definition 10.2.1 (因数与倍数)**
> 
> 在整数除法中, 如果商是整数且没有余数 (余数为 $0$), 就说除数是被除数的因数 (也称约数), 被除数是除数的倍数. 因数和倍数是相互依存的, 研究因数和倍数时所说的数一般指不包括 $0$ 的自然数.
> **Definition 10.2.2 (因数与倍数的特点)**
> 
> 一个数的因数的个数是有限的, 最小因数是 $1$, 最大因数是它本身; 一个数的倍数的个数是无限的, 最小倍数是它本身, 没有最大倍数.
> **Definition 10.2.3 (偶数与奇数)**
> 
> 整数中, 是 $2$ 的倍数的数叫作偶数 ($0$ 也是偶数), 不是 $2$ 的倍数的数叫作奇数.
> **Definition 10.2.4 (Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**
> 
> 个位是 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 的数是 2 的倍数; 个位是 0 或 5 的数是 5 的倍数; 各位上数字之和是 3 的倍数的数是 3 的倍数.
> **Definition 10.2.5 (质数与合数)**
> 
> 一个数, 如果只有 $1$ 和它本身两个因数, 叫作质数 (素数); 如果除了 $1$ 和它本身还有别的因数, 叫作合数. $1$ 既不是质数, 也不是合数.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 4) Put a digit in $\square$ so that $7\square$, $44\square$, $\square65$, $12\square$ are each multiples of $3$. How many choices each?

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**, the digit sum must be a multiple of $3$: $7\square$, $7+2=9$, choices $2,5,8$, $3$ ways; $44\square$, $8+1=9$, choices $1,4,7$, $3$ ways; $\square65$, $11+1=12$, choices $1,4,7$, $3$ ways; $12\square$, $3+0=3$, choices $0,3,6,9$, $4$ ways. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 5) Can you name $3$ even multiples of $3$? Can you name $3$ odd multiples of $5$?

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 10.2.3 (Even and Odd Numbers)** and **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**: even multiples of $3$ are $6,12,18$ (i.e. multiples of $6$); odd multiples of $5$ are $5,15,25$ (numbers ending in $5$). $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 6) (1) Counting "five, ten, fifteen, twenty, $\cdots$", the numbers counted are multiples of $\_\_$, and the $12$th number is $\_\_$; (2) $100,98,96,94,\cdots,8,6,4,2$, each number is a multiple of $\_\_$, and the $15$th number is $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**: (1) the counted numbers are multiples of $5$, and the $12$th is $5\times12=60$; (2) these are all even, multiples of $2$; the $1$st is $100$, and the $15$th is $100-2\times14=72$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 7) Mom bought some calla lilies and tulips at a flower shop; the clerk said she owed $87$ yuan. Calla lilies are $10$ yuan/stem, tulips $5$ yuan/stem, roses $3$ yuan/stem. Is the clerk right?

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**, the calla lily and tulip unit prices are $10$ and $5$ yuan, so the cost of these two flowers must be a multiple of $5$; but $87$ ends in neither $0$ nor $5$, so it is not a multiple of $5$, and the clerk is wrong. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 8) Judge whether the statements are correct and explain: (1) numbers ending in $3,6,9$ are all multiples of $3$; (2) numbers ending in $1,3,5,7,9$ are all odd; (3) among all integers, a number is either odd or even.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 10.2.3 (Even and Odd Numbers)** and **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**: (1) Wrong; multiples of $3$ depend on the digit sum, e.g. $13$ ends in $3$ but is not a multiple of $3$. (2) Correct; numbers ending in $1,3,5,7,9$ are not multiples of $2$. (3) Correct; integers split into odd and even by whether they are multiples of $2$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 9) There are $22$ students divided into groups to play; at least how many more students are needed to make groups of exactly $3$?

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**, find what to add to $22$ to make a multiple of $3$: $22+2=24$, $24\div3=8$, so at least $2$ more students. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 7

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 11) First circle the multiples of $4$ among $1\sim50$, then answer: (1) Are all multiples of $4$ also multiples of $2$? (2) Can you tell a multiple of $4$ just from its units digit?

### Solution to Exercise 8

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**: (1) Since $4$ is a multiple of $2$, every multiple of $4$ is a multiple of $2$; (2) You cannot tell from the units digit alone; look at the last two digits (a number whose last two digits form a multiple of $4$ is itself a multiple of $4$). $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Multiples of 2, 5, 3, Exercise 3, Problem 12) (1) The smallest two-digit number that is a multiple of $2$, $5$, and $3$ is $\_\_$; (2) The smallest three-digit multiple of both $2$ and $3$ is $\_\_$, and the largest three-digit one is $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**: (1) a multiple of $2,5,3$ at once is a multiple of $30$, so the smallest two-digit is $30$; (2) a multiple of $2,3$ at once is a multiple of $6$, so the smallest three-digit is $102$ and the largest three-digit is $996$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Prime and Composite Numbers, Exercise 4, Problem 3) Do you know what these numbers are? (1) Two primes with sum $10$ and product $21$; (2) two primes with sum $20$ and product $91$; (3) the smallest prime; (4) the smallest composite.

### Solution to Exercise 12

By **Definition 10.2.5 (Prime and Composite Numbers)**: (1) $3+7=10$, $3\times7=21$, so $3$ and $7$; (2) $7+13=20$, $7\times13=91$, so $7$ and $13$; (3) the smallest prime is $2$; (4) the smallest composite is $4$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Prime and Composite Numbers, Exercise 4, Problem 4) Is the product of an odd and an odd number odd or even? Odd times even? Even times even?

### Solution to Exercise 13

By **Definition 10.2.3 (Even and Odd Numbers)**: odd$\times$odd, neither factor is a multiple of $2$, so the product is not a multiple of $2$, i.e. odd; odd$\times$even, one factor is even, so the product is a multiple of $2$, i.e. even; even$\times$even, both factors contain $2$, so the product is a multiple of $2$, i.e. even. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Prime and Composite Numbers, Exercise 4, Problem 5) $30$ students are split into teams A and B. If team A has an odd number, is team B odd or even? What if team A is even?

### Solution to Exercise 14

By **Definition 10.2.3 (Even and Odd Numbers)**, since $30$ is even and odd$+$odd$=$even and even$+$even$=$even: if team A is odd, team B is also odd; if team A is even, team B is also even. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Prime and Composite Numbers, Exercise 4, Problem 6) In pairs, one says an even number greater than $2$, and the other finds two primes summing to it; e.g. for $10$, $3+7=10$.

### Solution to Exercise 15

By **Definition 10.2.5 (Prime and Composite Numbers)**, e.g. $4=2+2$, $6=3+3$, $8=5+3$, $10=7+3$, $12=7+5$, $14=11+3$, i.e. write an even number greater than $2$ as a sum of two primes. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Prime and Composite Numbers, Exercise 4, Problem 7) Explore the properties of multiples of $6$, and record your process and result.

### Solution to Exercise 16

By **Definition 10.2.4 (Properties of Multiples of 2, 5, 3)**, list $6,12,18,24,30,\cdots$ and observe: a multiple of $6$ is at once a multiple of $2$ (even units digit) and a multiple of $3$ (digit sum multiple of $3$); i.e. a number that is a multiple of both $2$ and $3$ is a multiple of $6$. $\blacksquare$
