> **Definition 10.1.1** (Directions for Observing Objects) <br>Viewing a solid built from identical small cubes from three directions — front, left, and top — yields a plane figure each; these three plane figures are called the three views of the solid. Viewing different solids from the same direction may yield the same plane figure.
> 
> **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views) <br>Building a solid from only one view does not give a unique arrangement; the solid's shape is determined only by the front, left, and top views. The number marked on each small square in the top view tells how many cubes stack at that position.

### Exercise 1

(Lesson: Observing Objects, Do It) Build a solid with $5$ identical small cubes as required: (1) From the front it looks like a row of $3$ small squares placed horizontally; (2) Building on (1), make the top view also a row of $3$ small squares placed horizontally.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views), first place $3$ cubes in a horizontal row so the front view is $3$ horizontal squares; then stack the remaining $2$ cubes above any two positions in the row, as long as the top view remains $3$ horizontal squares; the stacking positions differ, so the arrangement is not unique. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 2

(Lesson: Observing Objects, Exercise 1, Problem 1) The two problems below each show the views of the same solid from different directions; build the observed solid: (1) Given the front, left, and top views; (2) Given only the front and left views.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views), first build the bottom layer from the front view, then continue using the left view, and finally adjust against the top view. (1) With all three views, the solid is uniquely determined; (2) With only front and left views, the arrangement is not unique and has many possibilities. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(Lesson: Observing Objects, Exercise 1, Problem 3) Which solid meets the requirement "front view is $3$ horizontal squares, top view is the corresponding $2$ rows"? Mark "$\surd$" in the parentheses.

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views), only the solid satisfying both the front and top views meets the requirement; mark "$\surd$" in the parentheses below it, and not in the others. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 5

(Lesson: Observing Objects, Exercise 1, Problem 4) Viewing a solid made of identical small cubes from the front, it looks like $3$ horizontal squares. (1) If the solid has $4$ cubes, how can it be built? (2) If it has $5$, $6$, $7$, or more cubes, how can it be built?

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views), first place $3$ cubes in a horizontal row as the bottom layer, ensuring the front view is $3$ horizontal squares. (1) The $4$th cube may sit on top of any one of the three, giving $3$ arrangements. (2) Any extra cubes may be stacked on top of an existing position, as long as the front view remains $3$ horizontal squares; the number of arrangements grows with the count. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 6

(Lesson: Observing Objects, Exercise 1, Problem 5) Build a solid from identical cubes whose top view is a "$2$-row" shape, with numbers $1,3,1,2$ on the squares (telling how many cubes stack at each position). What does the front view and the left view look like?

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Definition 10.1.2** (Building a Solid from Views), the number on each square in the top view is the number of cubes stacked at that position. From the front, each column's height is the largest number among the positions in that column; from the left, each row's height is the largest number among the positions in that row. Draw the front and left views accordingly. $\blacksquare$
