> **Definition 11.5.1 (圆各部分的名称)**
> 
> 圆规针尖固定的一点叫圆心, 用 $O$ 表示; 连接圆心和圆上任意一点的线段叫半径, 用 $r$ 表示; 通过圆心并且两端都在圆上的线段叫直径, 用 $d$ 表示. 同一个圆里 $d=2r$. 墨子云: "圆, 一中同长也".

> **Definition 11.5.2 (弧, 扇形, 圆心角)**
> 
> 圆上两点之间的部分叫弧; 一条弧和经过这条弧两端的两条半径所围成的图形叫扇形; 顶点在圆心的角叫圆心角. 同一个圆中, 扇形的大小与圆心角的大小有关.

> **Definition 11.5.3 (圆周率)**
> 
> 任意一个圆的周长除以它的直径, 所得的商是一个固定的数, 叫圆周率, 用 $\pi$ 表示. 它是无限不循环小数, 计算时通常取 $\pi\approx3.14$.

> **Definition 11.5.4 (圆的周长)**
> 
> 围成圆的曲线的长叫圆的周长, $C=\pi d=2\pi r$.

> **Definition 11.5.5 (圆的面积)**
> 
> 圆所占平面的大小叫圆的面积. 把圆等分拼成近似长方形, 长为 $\pi r$, 宽为 $r$, 故 $S=\pi r^2$.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Do It Problem 2) Draw a circle of radius $2\,\text{cm}$ and mark its center, radius, and diameter with $O$, $r$, $d$.

The figure: a circle with center marked $O$, a segment from $O$ to a point on the circle labeled radius $r$, and a segment through $O$ with both endpoints on the circle labeled diameter $d$.

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Do It Problem 3) For a circle traced by a cup rim, how do you find the center?

### Solution to Exercise 2

Fold the circular paper twice; the intersection of the two creases (diameters) is the center. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Example 2) The part between points $A$, $B$ on a circle is an arc, read \"arc $AB$\". The figure bounded by an arc and the two radii to its endpoints is a sector. An angle like $\angle AOB$ with vertex at the center is a central angle.

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 11.5.2 (Arcs, Sectors, Central Angles)**, draw sectors of different sizes in the same circle and see that size depends on the central angle; a semicircle arc has central angle $180^{\circ}$, a quarter-circle arc $90^{\circ}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Review Problem 2) Fill the table (unit: m): given diameters $0.24$, $1.42$, $0.86$, $1.04$, find radii.

### Solution to Exercise 6

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Review Problem 3) Use a method to measure the diameter of a circle with no center marked.

### Solution to Exercise 7

Rest one leg of a set square against a ruler and use the other leg to grip the circle; slide until both sides touch the circle; the distance between the legs is the diameter (longest chord). $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Review Problem 4) Draw the other half of the axisymmetric figure from its axis of symmetry.

### Solution to Exercise 8

By axisymmetry, reflect each known vertex across the axis (same distance to the axis) and join in order to draw the other half. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Review Problem 5) Which angles below are central angles? Mark \"√\". Is a sector axisymmetric? How many axes?

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 11.5.2 (Arcs, Sectors, Central Angles)**, an angle with vertex at the center is central. A sector is axisymmetric with exactly $1$ axis (the line through center and arc midpoint). $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Integrated Problem 6) The school wants a circular flower bed of diameter $10\,\text{m}$; how can you draw it?

### Solution to Exercise 10

$r=5\,\text{m}$. Mark a point on the ground as center, fix a $5\,\text{m}$ rope at it, pull taut, and sweep a full circle. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Integrated Problem 7) Fill from the figure: cylinder height $3.5\,\text{cm}$, base diameter $10\,\text{cm}$; find $d$.

### Solution to Exercise 11

The rectangle holds two equal circles; its length $10\,\text{cm}$ is two diameters, so $d=10\div2=5\,\text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Integrated Problem 8) A rectangle holds three equal circles; its length is $18\,\text{cm}$. Circle diameter? Rectangle perimeter?

### Solution to Exercise 12

$18\,\text{cm}$ is $3$ diameters, $d=18\div3=6\,\text{cm}$; rectangle width equals the diameter $6\,\text{cm}$, perimeter $=\left(18+6\right)\times2=48\,\text{cm}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Integrated Problem 9) Draw axes of symmetry in each figure. Which plane figures we've studied are axisymmetric? Which have exactly one axis?

### Solution to Exercise 13

A circle has infinitely many axes; a square $4$, equilateral triangle $3$, rectangle $2$, isosceles triangle $1$, sector $1$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Understanding Circles, Exercise 10 Extension Problem 10) Using the three vertices of an equilateral triangle as centers and side length as radius, what pattern emerges?

### Solution to Exercise 14

The three circles overlap pairwise into a three-petal flower (each passes through the other two centers). $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Circumference, Example 3) The rim of a round table and chopping board needs an iron band. Measure several circles' circumference and diameter, divide circumference by diameter; what do you find?

### Solution to Exercise 15

A circle's circumference is always a bit more than $3$ times its diameter. By **Definition 11.5.3 (Pi)**, the quotient is the fixed number $\pi$. So circumference $C=\pi d=2\pi r$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Circumference, Example 4) A car wheel has diameter $75\,\text{cm}$. In $1$ revolution how far does the car go? If it travels $4710\,\text{km}$, how many wheel revolutions?

### Solution to Exercise 16

By **Definition 11.5.4 (Circumference)**, $C=3.14\times75=235.5\,\text{cm}=2.355\,\text{m}$. $4710\,\text{km}=4710000\,\text{m}$, $4710000\div2.355=2000000$ revolutions. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 17

(Lesson: Circumference, Do It Problem 1) Find circumferences: $d=8\,\text{cm}$; $r=3\,\text{cm}$; $r=5\,\text{cm}$. A tape measure gives a round tabletop perimeter $6.28\,\text{m}$; what's the diameter?

### Solution to Exercise 17

By **Definition 11.5.4 (Circumference)**: $3.14\times8=25.12\,\text{cm}$; $2\times3.14\times3=18.84\,\text{cm}$; $2\times3.14\times5=31.4\,\text{cm}$; diameter $6.28\div3.14=2\,\text{m}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 19

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Review Problem 2) In a circular pavilion, Xiaoli walks across the diameter in $12$ steps, each about $55\,\text{cm}$. About what is the circumference?

### Solution to Exercise 19

Diameter $=12\times55=660\,\text{cm}=6.6\,\text{m}$, circumference $=3.14\times6.6=20.724\,\text{m}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 20

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Review Problem 3) In an old building, a big red cylinder's cross-section perimeter is $3.14\,\text{m}$; what is its diameter?

### Solution to Exercise 20

$3.14\div3.14=1\,\text{m}$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 21

(Lesson: Circumference, Exercise 11 Review Problem 4) A unicyclist's wheel is $40\,\text{cm}$ diameter; to cross a $50.24\,\text{m}$ wire, about how many wheel revolutions?

### Solution to Exercise 21

Wheel circumference $3.14\times40=125.6\,\text{cm}=1.256\,\text{m}$; $50.24\div1.256=40$ revolutions. $\blacksquare$
