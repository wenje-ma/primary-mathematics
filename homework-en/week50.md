> **Definition 5.5.1 (口算乘法)**
> 
> 整十, 整百数乘一位数, 先把 $0$ 前面的数与一位数相乘, 再在积的末尾添上相应个数的 $0$. 两位数乘一位数, 把两位数分成整十数和一位数, 分别相乘后再相加.

> **Definition 5.5.2 (笔算乘法)**
> 
> 多位数乘一位数的竖式: 从个位起, 用一位数依次乘多位数的每一位; 哪一位上乘得的积满几十, 就向前一位进几.

> **Definition 5.5.3 (0 的乘法)**
> 
> $0$ 和任何数相乘都得 $0$.

> **Definition 5.5.4 (因数中间或末尾有 0 的乘法)**
> 
> 因数中间有 $0$, 这一位也要乘; 因数末尾有 $0$, 可以先乘 $0$ 前面的数, 再在积的末尾添上相同个数的 $0$.

> **Definition 5.5.5 (用估算解决问题)**
> 
> 有时只需判断"够不够", 不必精确计算, 可把数往大估或往小估: 把人数往大估, 钱数都够, 实际一定够.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 8) Are the following calculations correct? Correct the wrong ones: $12\times7=74$; $28\times3=44$; $183\times3=549$; $812\times4$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

$12\times7=84$ (the original $74$ is wrong); $28\times3=84$ (the original $44$ is wrong); $183\times3=549$ (correct); $812\times4=3248$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 9) A grasshopper's body is $5$ centimeters long, and the distance it jumps in one leap is $25$ times its body length. How far does it jump in one leap?

### Solution to Exercise 2

$5\times25=125$ centimeters. A person's long jump is generally less than $2$ meters ($200$ centimeters), so they can be compared. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 3

### Solution to Exercise 4

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 12) $400$ students go on a trip in $7$ vehicles; the first $6$ vehicles each seat $58$ students. How many students does the $7$th vehicle seat?

### Solution to Exercise 5

The first $6$ vehicles carry $58\times6=348$ students, and the $7$th vehicle carries $400-348=52$ students. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 13) The band has $18$ girls and $17$ boys; the chorus has $3$ times as many people as the band. How many people are in the chorus?

### Solution to Exercise 6

The band has $18+17=35$ people in total, and the chorus has $35\times3=105$ people. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 14) $14$ people go rowing; they rent $1$ boat for $6$ people and $2$ boats for $4$ people, for $2$ hours. A boat for $6$ people costs $30$ yuan per hour and a boat for $4$ people $25$ yuan per hour. How much is spent in total?

### Solution to Exercise 7

Per hour: $30+25\times2=80$ yuan; for $2$ hours: $80\times2=160$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 15) First-class carriages have $13$ rows with $4$ seats per row, and second-class has $18$ rows with $5$ seats per row. How many more seats does second-class have than first-class?

### Solution to Exercise 8

First-class has $13\times4=52$ seats; second-class has $18\times5=90$ seats; the difference is $90-52=38$ seats. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 16) Look at the line-segment diagrams, make up real-world problems, and write the equations: (1) one segment is $25$, and the other is $30$ times it? (2) A is $10$, B is some multiple of A, and together they total $48$?

### Solution to Exercise 9

(1) For example, apples are $25$ kilograms and pears are $30$ times the apples; how many pears: $25\times30=750$ kilograms. (2) A is $10$, A and B together are $48$, so B is $48-10=38$. Answers may vary. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: Practice 9, Problem 17, Challenge Problem) Observe the pattern: $99\times1=99$, $99\times2=198$, $99\times3=297$, $99\times4=396$, $99\times5=495$. Write down directly $99\times6$, $99\times7$, $99\times8$, $99\times9$.

### Solution to Exercise 10

Pattern: the hundreds digit of the product increases by $1$ each time, the ones digit decreases by $1$, and the tens digit is always $9$. $99\times6=594$, $99\times7=693$, $99\times8=792$, $99\times9=891$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Multiplication by 0 — Example 6) In $7$ plates, each plate has $0$ peaches; how many peaches in total?

### Solution to Exercise 11

$0+0+\cdots+0=0$, $0\times7=0$ peaches. By **Definition 5.5.3 (Multiplication by 0)**, $0$ multiplied by any number gives $0$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Multiplication by 0 — Do It Yourself) Mental math: $0\times2$, $5\times0$, $0\times6$, $0\times8$, $2\times0$, $5+0$, $6\times0$, $0+8$. Fill in the operation symbols in the $\bigcirc$: $4\bigcirc4=0$, $4\bigcirc4=4$, $0\bigcirc10=0$, $0\bigcirc0=0$, $4\bigcirc0=0$, $7\bigcirc7=0$.

### Solution to Exercise 13

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Multiplication with a 0 in a Factor — Example 7) The stands are divided into $8$ sections, each with $604$ seats. How many seats in total?

### Solution to Exercise 14

$604\times8=4832$ seats. By **Definition 5.5.4 (Multiplication with a 0 in the Middle or at the End of a Factor)**, estimate $600\times8=4800$; the tens $0\times8=0$ plus the $3$ carried from the ones gives $3$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Multiplication with a 0 in a Factor — Example 8) Buy $3$ sets of popular-science books at $280$ yuan per set. How much is spent in total?

### Solution to Exercise 15

$280\times3=840$ yuan. By **Definition 5.5.4 (因数中间或末尾有 0 的乘法)**, think of it as $28$ tens times $3$ gives $84$ tens, i.e. $840$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 17

(Lesson: Estimation — Example 9) The school organizes $987$ students to go to the botanical garden; admission is $8$ yuan per person. Is $8000$ yuan enough for the tickets?

### Solution to Exercise 17

By **Definition 5.5.5 (Solving Problems by Estimation)**, take $987$ as $1000$; since $987<1000$, $1000\times8=8000$, so $987\times8<8000$. $8000$ yuan is enough. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 18

(Lesson: Estimation — Do It Yourself) Roller skates are $218$ yuan per pair; Teacher Chen buys $5$ pairs. Is $1000$ yuan enough?

### Solution to Exercise 18

Take $218$ as $200$; $200\times5=1000$. Actually $218>200$, so $218\times5>1000$, and $218\times5=1090$ yuan. $1000$ yuan is not enough. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 19

(Lesson: Practice 10, Problem 1) Without calculating, say which result is larger: $1+2+3+\cdots+9+0$ versus $1\times2\times3\times\cdots\times9\times0$.

### Solution to Exercise 19

By **Definition 5.5.3 (Multiplication by 0)**, the multiplication expression ends with multiplying by $0$, so its product is $0$; the addition expression is at least $45$. The addition expression is larger. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 20

(Lesson: Practice 10, Problem 2) Fill in "$>$", "$<$" or "$=$": $8\times0\bigcirc8+0$; $14\times6\bigcirc16\times4$; $130\times0\bigcirc130-0$; $54-0\bigcirc54+0$; $400+9\bigcirc400\times9$; $304\times8\bigcirc2400$.

### Solution to Exercise 20

$8\times0=0<8+0=8$; $14\times6=84>16\times4=64$; $130\times0=0<130-0=130$; $54-0=54=54+0$; $400+9=409<400\times9=3600$; $304\times8=2432>2400$. $\blacksquare$
