> **Definition 4.1.1 (钟面的结构)**<br>钟面上有三根指针: 时针, 分针, 秒针, 还有格子和数. 钟面被数平均分成 $12$ 个大格, 每个大格又分成 $5$ 个小格, 钟面上一共有 $60$ 个小格.
> **Definition 4.1.2 (时,分,秒)**<br>时针转 $1$ 大格是 $1$ 时, 分针转 $1$ 小格是 $1$ 分, 秒针转 $1$ 小格是 $1$ 秒. 时, 分, 秒是记录时间的单位.
> **Definition 4.1.3 (Conversions of Time)**<br>时针转 $1$ 圈是 $12$ 时, 分针转 $1$ 圈是 $60$ 分, 秒针转 $1$ 圈是 $60$ 秒. $1$ 时 $=60$ 分, $1$ 分 $=60$ 秒. 生活中把 $30$ 分说成半小时, 把 $30$ 秒说成半分钟.
> **Definition 4.1.4 (认读几时几分)**<br>认读钟面上的时间, 先看时针指过几就是几时多, 再看分针从 $12$ 起转了多少个小格就是几分. 时针指向 $7$, 分针指向 $12$, 是 $7$ 时; 如 $4$ 时 $5$ 分, $4$ 时 $30$ 分 ($4$ 时半), $4$ 时 $45$ 分.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Where Is Time, Say) Day and night alternate, and a day has passed. Say how Xiaoyun's day is arranged.

### Solution to Exercise 1

Using your own daily routine, say in chronological order the times when Xiaoyun gets up, eats, goes to school, plays, and sleeps; for example get up at $7$ in the morning, arrive at school at $8$, leave school at $4$ in the afternoon, and go to bed at $9$ at night. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Telling Time, Observe) Look carefully; what is on the clock face? The face is divided into $\_\_$ big divisions, each big division has $\_\_$ small divisions, and the face has $\_\_$ small divisions in all.

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 4.1.1 (Structure of the Clock Face)**, the face has $3$ hands, divisions, and numbers. The face is divided into $12$ big divisions, each big division has $5$ small divisions, making $12\times5=60$ small divisions in all. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Telling Time, Turn the Hands) Turn the hands and see the relation among hours, minutes, and seconds. The hour hand going $1$ turn means $\_\_$ hours have passed; the minute hand going $1$ turn means $\_\_$ minutes have passed; the second hand going $1$ turn means $\_\_$ seconds have passed. $1$ hour $=60$ minutes, $1$ minute $=\_\_$ seconds. In daily life $30$ minutes is called half an hour and $30$ seconds half a minute; do you understand why?

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 4.1.3 (Rates Between Hours/Minutes and Minutes/Seconds)**, the hour hand going $1$ turn is $12$ hours, the minute hand going $1$ turn is $60$ minutes, and the second hand going $1$ turn is $60$ seconds. $1$ minute $=60$ seconds. $30$ minutes is exactly half of $60$ minutes, and $30$ seconds is exactly half of $60$ seconds, so they are called half an hour and half a minute. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Telling Time, Try It) Can you read the times on the clock faces below? (The three faces show $4$ hours $5$ minutes, $4$ hours $30$ minutes, and $4$ hours $45$ minutes.)

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 4.1.4 (Reading Hours and Minutes)**, the hour hand has passed $4$ in all, so it is a bit past $4$; then look at the minute hand: pointing to $1$ is $5$ minutes, to $6$ is $30$ minutes (half), to $9$ is $45$ minutes. Read respectively as $4$ hours $5$ minutes, $4$ hours $30$ minutes (or $4$ thirty), $4$ hours $45$ minutes. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Telling Time, Read) How do you read the time on the clock? Counting from $4$ o'clock, however many small divisions the minute hand has moved from $12$ gives the minutes past $4$.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 4.1.4 (Reading Hours and Minutes)**, first use the hour hand to determine the hour, then use the small divisions the minute hand has moved from $12$ to determine the minutes, and together they give the time. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: My Story with Time, Small Survey) What can you do in $1$ minute? Think, try, or survey. Reference: $50$ meters in $11$ seconds, one kilometer in $3$ minutes $50$ seconds, a marathon in $3$ hours; my pulse beats $88$ times in $1$ minute, I jump rope $99$ times in $1$ minute, an announcer reads about three hundred characters in $1$ minute, and a hummingbird's heart beats about six hundred times in $1$ minute.

### Solution to Exercise 7

Just actually do it or survey and fill in the table. ## Exercise 8

(Lesson: My Story with Time, Estimate) Count seconds along with the second hand, up to $1$ minute: $1,2,3,\cdots,59,60$.

### Solution to Exercise 8

Count one number each second along with the second hand, from $1$ to $60$, and you finish exactly $1$ minute. By **Definition 4.1.3 (Conversions of Time)**, $1$ minute $=60$ seconds. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: My Story with Time, Estimate) How long is $1$ second? What can you do in $1$ second? One "tick" of the clock is $1$ second, and a blink takes about $1$ second. A firefighter can climb from the ground to the $4$th floor on a hook ladder within $15$ seconds.

### Solution to Exercise 9

$1$ second is very short; one tick of the clock or one blink is about $1$ second. Short as $1$ second is, rescue and emergency care must race against every second; for example a firefighter can climb from the ground to the $4$th floor in $15$ seconds. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: My Story with Time, Estimate) Group activity: count seconds with your eyes closed, raise your hand when you reach $30$ seconds, and see who estimated most accurately. Then exchange estimating methods with classmates and estimate again after adjusting.

### Solution to Exercise 10

With your eyes closed, count one number per second in your head, and raise your hand at $30$. By **Definition 4.1.3 (Conversions of Time)**, $30$ seconds is half a minute. After exchanging with classmates you can adjust your counting rhythm, and the next estimate will be more accurate. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: My Little Time Book) Xiaoming made a little time book from photos, "A Day at the Ice-Snow Park": leaving home at $9:30$ in the morning, after arriving at the park he skis, then skates, eats lunch, and returns home at $2:30$ in the afternoon. Say what he did at each time. How long did it take from home to the park? From leaving home to returning, how long did it take altogether? What other time-related questions can you raise?

### Solution to Exercise 11

Leaving home at $9:30$ in the morning, he skis, skates, and eats lunch at the park, and returns home at $2:30$ in the afternoon. From $9:30$ a.m. to $2:30$ p.m., $5$ hours passed in all. The time from home to the park is found by subtracting the arrival time from the departure time. You can also raise questions such as "how long did lunch take?" $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

### Solution to Exercise 13

Just pick a question, research it, and present it.
