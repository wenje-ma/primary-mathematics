> **Definition 9.1.1 (视图)**
> 
> 从某一方向观察一个物体时, 所看到的平面图形叫作物体的一个视图. 按观察方向分为主视图 (从前面看), 俯视图 (从上面看), 左视图 (从左面看) 等.

> **Definition 9.1.2 (从同一方向观察不同组合体)**
> 
> 从同一方向观察不同的组合体, 看到的图形可能相同, 也可能不同; 从不同方向观察同一个组合体, 看到的图形一般也不同.

> **Definition 9.1.3 (Reconstructing from Views)**
> 
> 根据从前面, 上面, 左面三个方向看到的图形, 可以确定由小正方体摆成的组合体的形; 若只给出一个方向的视图, 组合体的摆法往往不唯一.

> **Definition 9.2.1 (小数乘整数)**
> 
> 先把小数转化为整数 (乘 $10,100,\cdots$), 按整数乘法算出积, 再把积除以相同的倍数, 点上小数点. 如 $9.5\times3$, 把 $9.5$ 看作 $95$ 角, $95\times3=285$ 角 $=28.5$ 元.

> **Definition 9.2.2 (小数乘小数)**
> 
> 先按整数乘法算出积, 再看两个乘数中一共有几位小数, 就从积的右边起数出几位, 点上小数点; 乘得的积的小数位数不够时, 要在前面用 $0$ 补足, 再点小数点.

> **Definition 9.2.3 (积的近似数)**
> 
> 根据需要, 按"四舍五入"法保留一定的小数位数, 求出积的近似数.

> **Definition 9.2.4 (倍数是小数)**
> 
> 一个数 ( $0$ 除外) 乘大于 $1$ 的数, 积比原来的数大; 乘小于 $1$ 的数, 积比原来的数小. 用小数表示倍的关系时, 也用乘法解决.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Example 3) Build the combination as required: (1) from the front, see two small squares of $1\times2$ side by side; (2) the front, top and left views are all given; build this combination.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 9.1.3 (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views)**, first build the basic shape from the front view, then adjust the cubes layer by layer according to the top and left views until all three views meet the requirements.

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 3) Among the following $3$ combinations, from which direction are the seen figures the same, and from which direction are they different?

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 9.1.2 (Observing Different Combinations from the Same Direction)**, compare direction by direction: same if the projections coincide, otherwise different.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 6

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Review and Consolidation, Problem 4) Use $5$ identical small cubes to build the combination as required: (1) from the front see two small squares of $1\times2$; (2) on the basis of (1), from above see the specified shape.

### Solution to Exercise 6

By **Definition 9.1.3 (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views)**, first arrange by the front shape, then adjust the position of the $5$th cube according to the top shape; there may be several arrangements.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Integrated Use, Problem 1) Below are some combinations built from identical small cubes. (1) Which look from the front like the specified shape, and which look from the left like the specified shape? (2) Observing from the front a combination built from $5$ cubes, the seen figure is the same as the front view of a known combination; how many different arrangements does this combination have?

### Solution to Exercise 7

By **Definition 9.1.2 (从同一方向观察不同组合体)** and **Definition 9.1.3 (Reconstructing from Views)**, examine each combination's front and left views and classify them; with the front view fixed, the $5$th cube can be placed in several positions that do not change the front view; enumerate them to get the number of arrangements.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Extension and Improvement, Problem 1) Observing from the front a combination built from identical small cubes, the seen figure is two small squares of $1\times2$. (1) If the combination has $4$ cubes, how can it be arranged? (2) If it has $5$, $6$, $7$ or more cubes, how can it be arranged?

### Solution to Exercise 8

By **Definition 9.1.3 (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views)**, with the front view fixed, extra cubes can only be placed where they do not change the front projection (stacked or offset along the front-back direction). With $4$ cubes there are already several arrangements; each added cube can be placed behind or above the existing combination without affecting the front view.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Extension and Improvement, Problem 2) Build a combination from identical cubes; in the top view the number on each square indicates how many cubes are used at that position. What does this combination look like from the front? From the left?

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 9.1.1 (Views)**, the top view fixes the bottom layout and the layers in each column; from the front take the maximum layer of each column to draw the column height, and from the left take the maximum layer of each row to draw the column height.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Multiplying a Decimal by an Integer, Example 1) An item costs $9.5$ yuan; how much do $3$ cost? $9.5\times3=\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 11

By **Definition 9.2.1 (Multiplying a Decimal by an Integer)**, $9.5$ yuan $=95$ jiao, $95\times3=285$ jiao $=28.5$ yuan. That is, $9.5\times3=28.5$ yuan.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 12

(Lesson: Multiplying a Decimal by an Integer, Example 2) Calculate $0.72\times60$.

### Solution to Exercise 12

By **Definition 9.2.1 (小数乘整数)**, expand $0.72$ by $100$ to get $72$, $72\times60=4320$, then divide by $100$ to get $43.20$, and remove the trailing $0$ to get $43.2$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Multiplying a Decimal by an Integer, Try It, Problem 1) An item costs $8.6$ yuan; how much do $6$ cost? Is $40$ yuan enough for $7$?

### Solution to Exercise 13

By **Definition 9.2.1 (小数乘整数)**, $6$ items: $8.6\times6=51.6$ yuan. $7$ items: $8.6\times7=60.2$ yuan; $60.2>40$, so not enough.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Multiplying a Decimal by a Decimal, Example 1) Class 4(1)'s campus test plot is $4.2$ m long and $0.8$ m wide; what is its area? $4.2\times0.8=\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 14

By **Definition 9.2.2 (Multiplying a Decimal by a Decimal)**, expand $4.2$ by $10$ to $42$ and $0.8$ by $10$ to $8$, $42\times8=336$; the two factors have two decimal places in total, so count two places from the right and place the point: $3.36$ m$^2$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Multiplying a Decimal by a Decimal, Try It) Calculate $1.92\times0.9$ and $0.32\times2.79$.

### Solution to Exercise 15

By **Definition 9.2.2 (小数乘小数)**: for $1.92\times0.9$, $192\times9=1728$, three decimal places in total, giving $1.728$. For $0.32\times2.79$, $32\times279=8928$, five decimal places in total, padding a $0$ on the front to get $0.8928$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 18

(Lesson: Approximate Value of a Product, Example) A human has about $0.049$ hundred million olfactory cells, and a dog has $45$ times as many. About how many hundred million olfactory cells does a dog have? (Keep one decimal.)

### Solution to Exercise 18

By **Definition 9.2.3 (Approximate Value of a Product)**, $0.049\times45=2.205$; keeping one decimal, look at the hundredths digit $0<5$, discard it, giving $2.2$ hundred million.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 19

(Lesson: Decimal Multiplication, Practice 2, Review and Consolidation, Problem 1) Place the decimal point in each product: $4.6\times30$, $0.46\times30$, $0.46\times300$ (the vertical products are $138$, $1380$, $13800$).

### Solution to Exercise 19

By **Definition 9.2.2 (小数乘小数)**: $4.6\times30$, one decimal place, gives $138.0=138$; $0.46\times30$, two decimal places, gives $13.80=13.8$; $0.46\times300$, two decimal places, gives $138.00=138$.

$\blacksquare$
