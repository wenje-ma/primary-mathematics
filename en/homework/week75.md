# Assignment 75

> **Definition 9.7.1** (Tessellation) <br>The phenomenon of covering the whole plane without overlaps or gaps between figures is called tessellation.
>
> **Definition 9.7.2** (Polygons That Tessellate) <br>A quadrilateral's interior-angle sum is $360^\circ$, and its four angles fit exactly around a vertex without overlap or gap, so any quadrilateral tessellates; a triangle's sum is $180^\circ$, and $6$ identical triangles fit together, so triangles tessellate; a regular pentagon has each interior angle $108^\circ$, which cannot make $360^\circ$, so it does not tessellate; circles do not tessellate.

### Exercise 183

(Lesson: Fascinating Tessellation, Example 1) What is tessellation? Give examples of tessellation in life.

### Solution to Exercise 183

By **Definition 9.7.1** (Tessellation), covering the whole plane without overlaps or gaps between figures is tessellation. In life, honeycombs, floor tiles and carpet patterns are all tessellations. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 184

(Lesson: Fascinating Tessellation, Try It, Problem 1) Choose a known figure and see whether it tessellates.

### Solution to Exercise 184

By **Definition 9.7.2**, parallelograms, trapezoids, triangles and quadrilaterals all tessellate; circles do not. Test by actually assembling them. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 185

By **Definition 9.7.2**, a quadrilateral's interior-angle sum is $360^\circ$, and around each vertex exactly four different angles fit together without overlap or gap. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 186

(Lesson: Fascinating Tessellation, Try It, Problem 3) Think: which other polygons tessellate and which do not?

### Solution to Exercise 186

By **Definition 9.7.2**, triangles ($180^\circ\times2=360^\circ$) and quadrilaterals ($360^\circ$) tessellate; a regular pentagon's interior angle is $108^\circ$, and $360\div108$ is not an integer, so it does not tessellate; circles do not tessellate. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 187

(Lesson: Fascinating Tessellation, Designing Patterns) Cut a piece from the bottom and left of a square and attach each to the top and right; the resulting pattern can tessellate. Try it.

### Solution to Exercise 187

By **Definition 9.7.1** and knowledge of figure motion, translate and rotate the cut pieces onto the opposite sides; the new figure keeps the tessellation property and can cover the plane continuously. $\blacksquare$
