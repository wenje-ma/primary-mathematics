> **Definition 9.1.1** (Views) <br>When observing an object from a certain direction, the planar figure seen is called a view of the object. By viewing direction it is divided into the front view (seen from the front), the top view (seen from above), the left view (seen from the left), and so on.
> 
> **Definition 9.1.2** (Observing Different Combinations from the Same Direction) <br>Observing different combinations from the same direction, the seen figures may be the same or different; observing the same combination from different directions, the seen figures are generally also different.
> 
> **Definition 9.1.3** (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views) <br>From the figures seen from the front, top and left, the shape of a combination built from small cubes can be determined; if only one direction's view is given, the arrangement is often not unique.

### Exercise 1

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Example 1) Build a combination from $4$ identical small cubes, with every cube in the combination sharing at least one face with another cube. How many different arrangements do you have?

### Solution to Exercise 1

First line up the $4$ small cubes in a row, then stack one on top of another or offset them front and back, giving many different arrangements, as long as adjacent cubes share at least one face. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 3

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Example 2) Build and look: there are $3$ different combinations; are the figures seen from above the same? What about from the left and front?

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 9.1.2** (Observing Different Combinations from the Same Direction), the three combinations look the same from above and the same from the left, but not the same from the front. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 4

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Try It, Problem 2) Build and look: among these $3$ combinations, from which direction are the seen figures the same, and from which direction are they different?

### Solution to Exercise 4

Actually build and observe: if the projections of the three combinations coincide in a direction, the seen figure is the same there; otherwise it is different. Judge according to the given combinations. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 5

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Example 3) Build the combination as required: (1) from the front, see two small squares of $1\times2$ side by side; (2) the front, top and left views are all given; build this combination.

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 9.1.3** (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views), first build the basic shape from the front view, then adjust the cubes layer by layer according to the top and left views until all three views meet the requirements. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 11

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Integrated Use, Problem 1) Below are some combinations built from identical small cubes. (1) Which look from the front like the specified shape, and which look from the left like the specified shape? (2) Observing from the front a combination built from $5$ cubes, the seen figure is the same as the front view of a known combination; how many different arrangements does this combination have?

### Solution to Exercise 11

By **Definition 9.1.2** and **Definition 9.1.3**, examine each combination's front and left views and classify them; with the front view fixed, the $5$th cube can be placed in several positions that do not change the front view; enumerate them to get the number of arrangements. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 12

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Extension and Improvement, Problem 1) Observing from the front a combination built from identical small cubes, the seen figure is two small squares of $1\times2$. (1) If the combination has $4$ cubes, how can it be arranged? (2) If it has $5$, $6$, $7$ or more cubes, how can it be arranged?

### Solution to Exercise 12

By **Definition 9.1.3** (Reconstructing a Combination from Its Views), with the front view fixed, extra cubes can only be placed where they do not change the front projection (stacked or offset along the front-back direction). With $4$ cubes there are already several arrangements; each added cube can be placed behind or above the existing combination without affecting the front view. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 13

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Extension and Improvement, Problem 2) Build a combination from identical cubes; in the top view the number on each square indicates how many cubes are used at that position. What does this combination look like from the front? From the left?

### Solution to Exercise 13

By **Definition 9.1.1** (Views), the top view fixes the bottom layout and the layers in each column; from the front take the maximum layer of each column to draw the column height, and from the left take the maximum layer of each row to draw the column height. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 14

(Lesson: Observing Simple Combinations, Practice 1, Extension and Improvement, Problem 3) Among the four options on the right, which combination is different from the one on the left?

### Solution to Exercise 14

By **Definition 9.1.2** (Observing Different Combinations from the Same Direction), compare the left combination with each option from the front, top and left; all three views must match for it to be the same combination, and any differing direction makes it a different combination. $\blacksquare$
