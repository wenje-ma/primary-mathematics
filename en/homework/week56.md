# Assignment 56

> **Definition 7.8.1** (The Eight Directions) <br>Besides the four directions east, south, west and north, there are four more: northeast, northwest, southeast and southwest. Northeast lies between east and north, southeast between east and south, northwest between west and north, and southwest between west and south. Northeast is opposite to southwest, and northwest is opposite to southeast.
>
> **Definition 7.8.2** (Position and Direction) <br>Describing an object's position requires a direction and a reference point (landmark). When drawing a plan map, mark the direction (north up, south down, west left, east right) and the positions of the landmarks. A compass can be used to tell directions.

### Exercise 213

(Lesson: Campus Treasure Hunt, Discuss) The treasure map marks the cafeteria, the teaching building, the multi-purpose hall, the gymnasium, the library, the playground, the national flag, the bicycle shed, the science and technology hall, and the main gate. Treasure-hunt instructions: Treasure No. $1$ is on the $2$nd floor of the middle building on the north side of campus, in the $1$st classroom counted from the west, $1$ m from the front blackboard; Treasure No. $3$ is under the largest tree at the southeast corner of the science and technology hall. What does "southeast corner" mean?

### Solution to Exercise 213

By **Definition 7.8.1** (The Eight Directions), the southeast corner is the direction between east and south, i.e. the lower-right of the science and technology hall. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 214

(Lesson: Recognize It) Observe the compass. What new findings do you have? Taking the compass and the treasure map, see which buildings lie in the school's southeast, southwest, northeast and northwest directions.

### Solution to Exercise 214

By **Definition 7.8.1** (The Eight Directions) and **Definition 7.8.2** (Position and Direction), the red needle of the compass points north and the white needle points south; between east and north is northeast, between west and south is southwest, and so on. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 215

### Solution to Exercise 215

By **Definition 7.8.2** (Position and Direction), first find the reference point, then mark the treasure positions according to direction and distance. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 216

(Lesson: Drawing the Treasure Map) If you are the person who hides the treasure, draw a campus treasure map. When drawing, mark the direction and the treasure positions.

### Solution to Exercise 216

By **Definition 7.8.2** (Position and Direction), mark a "north" arrow on the map, draw each landmark, and label the treasure numbers and their positions. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 217

(Lesson: Treasure-Hiding and Treasure-Hunting Challenge) Hide the treasure according to the best treasure map and instructions, then exchange maps with other groups to hunt. Interview the best treasure-hunting group about how they found the treasure quickly.

### Solution to Exercise 217

By **Definition 7.8.2** (Position and Direction), the key to fast treasure hunting is clear instructions, unambiguous landmarks, and accurate direction judgment. $\blacksquare$

### Exercise 218

(Lesson: Mini Lecture) Directions can also be described by the o'clock convention: the $12$ o'clock direction is straight ahead, $3$ o'clock is straight to the right, $6$ o'clock is straight behind, and $9$ o'clock is straight to the left.

### Solution to Exercise 218

By **Definition 7.8.1** (The Eight Directions), take the standing person's front as $12$ o'clock and go clockwise $30^{\circ}$ for each hour mark; for example, the $1$ o'clock direction is $30^{\circ}$ to the front-right. $\blacksquare$
