> **Definition 4.6.1** (Math Picture-Strip) <br>Use several pictures connected in order to tell a story that contains math information or quantitative relations; each picture has a caption, and a math problem can be raised from it. For example, in the pictures mark the order of each frame.
>
> **Definition 4.6.2** (Division Situations in the Picture-Strip) <br>Express the quantitative relations in the picture-strip story by an expression. For example bought $11$ cans, eating $5$ per day, $11\div5=2$(days)……$1$(can), meaning enough for $2$ days, with $1$ can left.

### Exercise 198

(Lesson: Picture-Strip Sharing) Xiaodong's strip "The Kitten Buys Cans": the kitten family goes to the supermarket, buys $11$ cans and goes home happily, and they eat $5$ cans each day. What math knowledge does this strip contain? Eating $5$ cans a day, for how many days is it enough? How many cans will the kitten family eat in $2$ days?

### Solution to Exercise 198

By **Definition 4.6.2** (Division Situations in the Picture-Strip), $11\div5=2$(days)……$1$(can), enough for $2$ days, with $1$ can left; in $2$ days they eat $5\times2=10$ cans. The strip contains division with remainder and multiplication. $\blacksquare$
