> **Definition 12.1.1 (Positive and Negative Numbers)**
> 
> 像 3, 1.8%, 3.5 这样大于 0 的数叫做正数; 像 -3, -2.7%, -4.5 这样在正数前加上负号的数叫做负数.

> **Definition 12.2.1 (折扣)**
> 
> 商品按原价的百分之几出售, 通常称打几折出售. 几折就表示十分之几, 也就是百分之几十.

> **Definition 12.5.3 (正比例)**
> 
> 两种相关联的量, 一种量变化, 另一种量也随着变化, 如果这两种量中相对应两个数的比值一定, 这两种量叫做成正比例的量.

> **Definition 12.5.4 (反比例)**
> 
> 两种相关联的量, 一种量变化, 另一种量也随着变化, 如果这两种量中相对应两个数的乘积一定, 这两种量叫做成反比例的量.

> **Definition 12.5.5 (比例尺)**
> 
> 一幅图的图上距离和实际距离的比, 叫做这幅图的比例尺. 比例尺 = 图上距离 / 实际距离.

> **Definition 12.6.1 (自行车里的数学)**
> 
> 脚踏板带动前齿轮, 前齿轮带动后齿轮, 后齿轮带动后轮转动. 前齿轮齿数 $\times$ 前齿轮转数 $=$ 后齿轮齿数 $\times$ 后齿轮转数, 即后齿轮转数 $=\frac{\text{前齿轮齿数}}{\text{后齿轮齿数}}\times$ 前齿轮转数. 蹬一圈走的距离 $=\frac{\text{前齿轮齿数}}{\text{后齿轮齿数}}\times$ 车轮周长. 蹬同样圈数, 前后齿轮齿数的比值越大, 自行车走得越远.

> **Definition 12.7.1 (鸽巢原理)**
> 
> 把多于 $n$ 个物体放进 $n$ 个抽屉, 总有一个抽屉里至少放进 $2$ 个物体. 把多于 $kn$ 个物体放进 $n$ 个抽屉, 总有一个抽屉至少放进 $k+1$ 个物体. 计算方法: 用物体数除以抽屉数, 若得商 $k$ 余 $r$ ($r>0$), 则总有一个抽屉至少有 $k+1$ 个物体.

> **Definition 12.8.1 (数的分类与意义)**
> 
> 整数包括正整数, $0$, 负整数; 自然数是正整数和 $0$. 分数包括有限小数和无限循环小数. $0$ 既不是正数也不是负数. 把单位 $1$ 平均分成若干份, 表示这样一份或几份的数叫分数; 表示一个数是另一个数的百分之几的数叫百分数.

> **Definition 12.8.2 (运算律)**
> 
> 加法交换律 $a+b=b+a$; 加法结合律 $\left(a+b\right)+c=a+\left(b+c\right)$; 乘法交换律 $ab=ba$; 乘法结合律 $\left(ab\right)c=a\left(bc\right)$; 乘法分配律 $\left(a+b\right)c=ac+bc$. 利用运算律可使计算简便.

> **Definition 12.8.3 (方程)**
> 
> 含有未知数的等式叫方程. 等式两边加上或减去同一个数, 左右两边仍然相等; 等式两边乘同一个数, 或除以同一个不为 $0$ 的数, 左右两边仍然相等.

> **Definition 12.8.4 (常见图形周长面积体积公式)**
> 
> 长方形 $C=2\left(a+b\right),S=ab$; 正方形 $C=4a,S=a^{2}$; 平行四边形 $S=ah$; 三角形 $S=\frac{1}{2}ah$; 梯形 $S=\frac{1}{2}\left(a+b\right)h$; 圆 $C=2\pi{r},S=\pi{r}^{2}$; 长方体 $V=abh$; 正方体 $V=a^{3}$; 圆柱 $V=Sh$; 圆锥 $V=\frac{1}{3}Sh$.

## Exercise 1

(Lesson: Activity 1) For a bicycle, how far does it travel in one pedal revolution? Given the front chainring has $48$ teeth, the rear sprocket has $16$ teeth, and the wheel diameter is $60\,\mathrm{cm}$.

### Solution to Exercise 1

By **Definition 12.6.1 (Mathematics in a Bicycle)**, the rear sprocket (rear wheel) makes $=\frac{48}{16}=3$ revolutions. The wheel circumference $=\pi{d}=3.14\times60=188.4\,\mathrm{cm}$. One pedal revolution travels $3\times188.4=565.2\,\mathrm{cm}\approx5.65\,\mathrm{m}$.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 2

(Lesson: Activity 2) A geared bicycle has front chainrings of $48$ and $40$ teeth and rear sprockets of $28,24,20,18,16,14$ teeth. (1) How many different tooth ratios can be combined? (2) With the same number of pedal revolutions, which combination makes the bicycle travel farthest?

### Solution to Exercise 2

By **Definition 12.6.1 (Mathematics in a Bicycle)**. (1) There are $2$ front chainrings and $6$ rear sprockets, giving $2\times6=12$ combinations. (2) With the same number of pedal revolutions, the larger the front-to-rear tooth ratio, the farther it travels; the largest ratio is front $48$ paired with rear $14$, i.e. $48:14$, and this combination travels farthest.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Example 1) Put $4$ pencils into $3$ pencil cups; however you distribute them, there is always $1$ cup containing at least $2$ pencils. Why?

### Solution to Exercise 3

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**, $4\div3=1\cdots\cdots1$, quotient $1$ remainder $1$. If each cup holds at most $1$ pencil, the $3$ cups hold at most $3$ pencils, and the remaining $1$ pencil must go into one of the cups; hence there is always $1$ cup with at least $1+1=2$ pencils.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Do It) Pick $13$ teachers at random; at least $2$ of them share the same Chinese zodiac sign. Why?

### Solution to Exercise 4

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**, there are $12$ zodiac signs, treated as $12$ drawers, and the $13$ teachers are $13$ objects. Since $13\div12=1\cdots\cdots1$, at least $1+1=2$ people share a sign.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 5

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Example 2) Put $7$ books into $3$ drawers; however you distribute them, there is always $1$ drawer containing at least $3$ books. Why? What if there are $8$ books, or $10$ books?

### Solution to Exercise 5

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**. $7\div3=2\cdots\cdots1$, so at least $2+1=3$ books. $8\div3=2\cdots\cdots2$, so at least $2+1=3$ books. $10\div3=3\cdots\cdots1$, so at least $3+1=4$ books.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 7

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Do It) After removing the jokers from a deck of cards, $52$ cards remain. $9$ people each draw $1$ card at random; at least $3$ cards are of the same suit. Why?

### Solution to Exercise 7

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**, the $52$ cards fall into $4$ suits, treated as $4$ drawers. The $9$ people draw $9$ cards, and $9\div4=2\cdots\cdots1$, so at least $2+1=3$ cards are of the same suit.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 8

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Example 3) A box contains red and blue balls, $4$ of each, of the same size. To be certain of drawing $2$ balls of the same color, at least how many balls must be drawn?

### Solution to Exercise 8

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**, there are $2$ colors, treated as $2$ drawers. In the worst case, first draw $2$ balls of different colors; the next $1$ draw must match one of them, so at least $2+1=3$ balls are needed.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 9

(Lesson: Pigeonhole Problems, Do It) Grade 6 of Dongxiang Elementary School has $367$ students in total, and Class 6(2) has $37$ students. Someone says that at least $2$ students in Grade 6 share a birthday, and at least $4$ students in Class 6(2) share a birth month. Is he right?

### Solution to Exercise 9

By **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**. A year has at most $366$ days, and $367\div366=1\cdots\cdots1$, so at least $1+1=2$ people share a birthday — correct. A year has $12$ months, and $37\div12=3\cdots\cdots1$, so at least $3+1=4$ people share a birth month — correct.

$\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 11

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Exercise 13, Problem 4) Given any $3$ different natural numbers, there must be $2$ whose sum is even. Explain why.

### Solution to Exercise 14

Natural numbers fall into two classes by parity: odd and even, treated as $2$ drawers. Putting $3$ natural numbers into $2$ drawers, by **Definition 12.7.1 (Pigeonhole Principle)**, at least $2$ numbers are both odd or both even. Odd $+$ odd $=$ even, and even $+$ even $=$ even, so the sum of these two numbers must be even.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Exercise 14, Problem 1) Fill in the blanks. (1) The highest temperature $49.7\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is recorded as $\_\_$, and the lowest temperature, $52.3\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ below zero, is recorded as $\_\_$. (2) If $a\div{b}=c$ ($a,b,c$ are integers, $b\neq0$), then the greatest common factor of $a$ and $b$ is $\_\_$, and the least common multiple is $\_\_$. (3) A commodity is sold at 30% off; "30% off" means the current price is $\_\_\%$ of the original price, and on a $100$-yuan original price you pay $\_\_$ yuan less.

### Solution to Exercise 15

By **Definition 12.1.1 (Positive and Negative Numbers)**, **Definition 12.8.1 (Classification and Meaning of Numbers)**, and **Definition 12.2.1 (Discount)**. (1) Above zero is recorded as positive: $+49.7\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$; below zero as negative: $-52.3\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. (2) Since $a$ is a multiple of $b$, the greatest common factor is $b$ and the least common multiple is $a$. (3) 30% off means $70\%$; you pay $100\times\left(1-70\%\right)=30$ yuan less.

$\blacksquare$

## Exercise 16

(Lesson: Exercise 14, Problem 4) Using the digits $2,3,4,5$, how many two-digit numbers with no repeated digits can be formed? Among these two-digit numbers, which are odd and which are even? Which are multiples of $2,3,5$?

### Solution to Exercise 16

The tens digit has $4$ choices and the units digit has $3$ left, giving $4\times3=12$ two-digit numbers. The odd ones end in $3$ or $5$: $23,43,53,25,35,45$; the even ones end in $2$ or $4$: $32,42,52,24,34,54$. The multiples of $2$ are the even ones above; the multiples of $3$, whose digit sum is a multiple of $3$, are $24,42,45,54$; the multiples of $5$, ending in $0$ or $5$, are $25,35,45$.

$\blacksquare$
