> **Definition 4.6.1 (数学连环画)**
> 
> 用几幅按顺序连起来的图画, 讲述一个包含数学信息或数量关系的故事, 每幅图配文字说明, 并能从中提出数学问题. 如画中要标出每幅画的顺序.

> **Definition 4.6.2 (连环画中的除法情境)**
> 
> 把连环画故事里的数量关系用算式表示. 如买了 $11$ 个罐头, 每天吃 $5$ 个, $11\div5=2$(天)... $1$(个), 表示够吃 $2$ 天, 还剩 $1$ 个.

> **Definition 4.7.1 (数与运算的整理)**
> 
> 在个 (一), 十, 百的基础上又认识了千, 万, 相邻计数单位间都是 $10$ 倍; 万以内数的组成, 读写, 顺序, 比大小, 近似数的方法与百以内数一致.

> **Definition 4.7.2 (除法与乘除关系的整理)**
> 
> 平均分没有剩余用表内除法, 有剩余用有余数的除法, 余数要比除数小; 求一个数是另一个数的几倍用除法, 求一个数的几倍是多少用乘法, 关键是分清把谁看作 $1$ 份.

> **Definition 4.7.3 (加,减法关系的整理)**
> 
> 加法和减法互为逆运算: 和 $=$ 加数 $+$ 加数, 被减数 $=$ 减数 $+$ 差, 利用这些关系可以验算和求未知数.

## Exercise 3

(Lesson: Applying and Improving) Use $0,3,6,8$ to make four-digit numbers. (1) The largest four-digit number is $\_\_$, its approximate number is $\_\_$; the smallest four-digit number is $\_\_$, its approximate number is $\_\_$ (rounded to a round thousand). (2) A four-digit number that does not read "zero" may be $\_\_$; one that reads one "zero" may be $\_\_$. (3) Show the largest and smallest four-digit numbers on a counter, and say how many counting units are in each place. (4) The computer price is made of $0,3,6,8$ and is a round ten; guess what it is.

### Solution to Exercise 3

(1) Largest $8630\approx9000$, smallest $3068\approx3000$. (2) Not reading zero, e.g. $3680$ (trailing $0$ is not read); reading one zero, e.g. $3068$ (the middle $0$ reads as one zero). (4) From the dialogue: $3680$ guessed too low, $8360$ too high, $6830$ a little high, so the price is $6380$ yuan. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 4

(Lesson: Applying and Improving) May Day museum attendance: month $5$, day $1$ $998$ people, day $2$ $1020$ people, day $3$ $985$ people. (1) What round thousand is each day close to? About how many people in three days? (2) Raise one addition and one subtraction problem and solve them. (3) After sharing $7$ bottles of mineral water equally among the family, $3$ bottles are left; how many people are in Xiaoming's family? (4) Draw the closing time on the clock (closes in $20$ minutes). (5) The museum visit took $2$ hours, and the time at the children's park plus the museum is $3$ times the museum time; how long at the children's park?

### Solution to Exercise 4

(1) $998\approx1000$, $1020\approx1000$, $985\approx1000$, about $3000$ people in three days. (2) E.g. how many in all (add), and which day had how many more (subtract). (3) $7\div$ number of people $=\cdots\cdots3$, the number of people divides $7-3=4$, shared equally with no remainder, $7\div4=1\cdots\cdots3$, a family of $4$. (5) Total time $2\times3=6$ hours, so the children's park took $6-2=4$ hours. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 10

(Lesson: Practice) A triangle ruler costs $4$ yuan, a pencil case $16$ yuan, and the price of the markers and the pen is unknown. (1) How many times the ruler is the pencil case? (2) The markers are $9$ times the ruler; how much is a box of markers? (3) The pencil case is $2$ times the pen; how much is a pen? (4) Raise other problems and solve them.

### Solution to Exercise 10

## Exercise 11

(Lesson: Practice) Pack apples as in the picture. (1) How many boxes can be filled? (2) If all apples go into boxes, how many boxes are needed in all?

### Solution to Exercise 11

(1) Divide the total apples by the number per box; the quotient is the number of filled boxes. (2) If some apples are left over, $1$ more box is needed, so boxes needed are the quotient $+1$. Compute per the apples and per-box count in the picture. $\blacksquare$

### Solution to Exercise 12

## Exercise 13

(Lesson: Practice) The number line is marked with $0,1000,5000$. (1) By the pattern, fill the numbers in the $\square$. (2) Starting at $0$, jump one division each time; after $5$ jumps you land on $\_\_$; to reach $10000$ you must jump $\_\_$ more times. (3) Another jump takes $10$ jumps to reach $1000$; fill the numbers in the $\square$.

### Solution to Exercise 13

From the gaps between $0,1000,5000$ on the line, each big division stands for $1000$; fill each $\square$ in order. (2) $5$ jumps reach $5000$, and to reach $10000$ need $5$ more jumps. (3) $10$ jumps reach $1000$, so each jump is $100$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 14

(Lesson: Practice) There are $8$ bowls and $14$ chopsticks; each guest gets one bowl and one pair of chopsticks; how many guests can these bowls and chopsticks serve?

### Solution to Exercise 14

$14$ chopsticks make $14\div2=7$ pairs, and there are $8$ bowls. Limited by the chopsticks, they can only serve $7$ guests. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 15

(Lesson: Practice) Choose from the four numbers $324,699,989,78$. (1) The two numbers whose "sum" is closest to $1000$ are $\_\_$ and $\_\_$. (2) The two numbers whose "difference" is closest to $600$ are $\_\_$ and $\_\_$.

### Solution to Exercise 15

(1) Try adding: $324+699=1023$ is closest to $1000$. (2) $989-324=665$, $699-78=621$ closest to $600$, so choose $699$ and $78$. $\blacksquare$

## Exercise 17

## Exercise 18

(Lesson: Practice) In a dart game, the board has regions worth $1,10,100,1000$ points; Xiaoli, Xiaoliang, and Xiaodong each throw some times, and Xiaodong has one last throw left. (1) Xiaoli's and Xiaoliang's scores are $\_\_$ and $\_\_$ points, and $\_\_$ scored better. (2) For Xiaodong to beat both, how many points must the last throw score at least?

### Solution to Exercise 18

(1) Multiply each person's hits in $1,10,100,1000$ regions by the counts, add, and compare. (2) Xiaodong's current score plus the last throw must exceed the higher of the two; work out the minimum extra points needed. $\blacksquare$
